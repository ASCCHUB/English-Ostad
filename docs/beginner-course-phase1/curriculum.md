# Beginner English for Pashto-speaking children — Phase 1

Draft for approval · 26 October 2026 · Ages 4–9 · 79 core lessons · Five levels

**Phase 2 is not authorised. No running app code is changed by this design.** Every English and Pashto entry and recording is pending named reviewer sign-off.

## Read this first

- Alphabet browsing is A–Z; sound lessons follow a systematic sound order. Starting with alphabetical A,B,C is not required for blending.
- No untaught correspondences applies to independent reading/spelling and scored tasks. Oral modeled picture words and letter names necessarily contain later sounds. Picture labels remain hidden until decodable or explicitly taught as an exception/name. This interpretation requires Phase 1 approval.
- The English lesson titles, objectives and comprehension-question glosses are authoring metadata. Child instructions/questions are Pashto; learner English print is explicitly listed practice content.
- The core course is cumulative, not a claim to cover all English spelling. Unlisted words are excluded from independent texts until introduced.
- No finite multiple-choice check can make chance success mathematically impossible. Gate on correct first responses and constructed output, use fresh retry forms, and flag repeated guessing; do not count watching/tapping Continue as mastery.

## Language, phonics and recording decisions

Short everyday phrases; polite plural تاسو / وکړئ matching the existing guide; familiar school/household vocabulary; no Afghan-register substitution.

The running app is React/Vite. Its existing Lower Dir style guide uses polite plural address; this draft follows that guide rather than introducing a new dialect label. A separate externally approved guide was not attached. Pashto translations below are drafts, not native approval. English glosses are for editors; the child hears Pashto task instructions and English practice targets.

The alphabet browser displays A–Z in order. The teaching order is: **s, a, t, p, i, n, m, d, g, o, c, k, e, u, r, h, b, f, l, j, v, w, x, y, z, qu**. Group sizes are 5, 5, 4, 4, 4, 4. Blend at/sat in the T lesson, before the alphabet is complete. Letter names and pure sound clips are separate controls and separate files. Q is shown with U for /k/ + /w/; X is /k/ + /s/ in box, never “x is for xylophone”.

One trained English speaker records clipped stops and sustainable consonants without an added vowel. The two th sounds and two oo sounds have separate entries. ar is an r-controlled pattern in the chosen model, not a generic long-vowel team. Capital letters do not introduce new sound correspondences. bath is an explicitly tagged accent listening example, excluded from scored short-a decoding.

## Every lesson: activities, assessment and gentle feedback

The JSON expands each lesson into its own activities and five check blueprints. Letter lessons alternate example, separate name/sound buttons, case matching, find, trace/tap-dots, sound-picture choice, independent check, celebration. Later lessons alternate modeling, listening, blending/building/reading, a meaning game, independent construction, check and celebration. No two adjacent activity types are identical. One skill goal governs each lesson; review and vocabulary introduction support that goal.

**Error 1:** ښه کوشش مو وکړ. بیا یې وګورئ.

**Error 2 / model:** راځئ، یو ځای یې وکړو. بیا به نوی مثال پخپله وکړئ.

**Failed check:** هیڅ خبره نه ده. لږ نور مشق به وکړو.

**Success:** use the lesson’s specific English target/achievement and its scripted Pashto success message. Full native-approved, lesson-specific celebration recordings are a release requirement; generic praise alone is not the final implementation.

Core assessment answer positions must be randomized. Random tapping cannot count as “practice completed”; checks score correct first responses, assisted items are not mastery, and constructed responses are required. Mathematical immunity to chance in a finite choice quiz cannot be promised. Speaking and tracing are not automatically machine-scored for literacy mastery.

## Activity checkpoint, offline and sync design

First unfinished activity in the first unlocked unfinished lesson; restore its in-activity cursor and committed answers.

Persist locally after every accepted interaction and activity commit, before changing activity. Resume paused; replay current short instruction from the start. Never auto-submit an unfinished answer.

Proposed IndexedDB transaction + durable sync outbox; retain existing profile identifiers and storage migration. Phase 2 storage implementation is not yet authorised.

Retry idempotent events on reconnection; remote account/provider is undecided. Anonymous offline use remains supported. Never silently replace newer per-activity work.

Checkpoint payload fields: learnerId, courseVersion, lessonId, activityId, activityContentVersion, promptIndex, placedTileIds, firstResponses, assistedItemIds, audioPositionMs, attemptId, updatedAt, completionEventId.

Existing parent gate; placement probes start below proposed entry, assess letters/blending/spelling/phrases/reading, require 80% unassisted with constructed responses. Parent records a placement decision; a class selector alone is not proof of mastery.

## Audio production and timing

The separate audio-manifest.json lists every referenced English word/sentence, letter name, pure sound and Pashto instruction with stable ID, language, recording notes, pending file, pending alignment and reviewer fields. No audio has been recorded by this design. Native reviewed recordings, not reading IPA through speech synthesis, are required for phonics. Clip-end and delivered word/phoneme timing drive animation; no fake timing or pretend synchronisation. One playback owner cancels old clips and stale callbacks, including Pashto help, global mute, replay, interruption and page navigation.

## Reviewer checklist

- [ ] Native English reviewer checks every sound-to-spelling mapping, article/pronoun pronunciation, sentence and literal answer key.
- [ ] Trained phonics reviewer checks cumulative independent word lists, pure consonants, two oo sounds, two th sounds, x/qu sequences, adjacent consonants and split digraphs.
- [ ] Lower Dir native reviewer checks every Pashto line for child comprehension, local vocabulary, respectful plural register, natural delivery and text direction.
- [ ] Review Ali/Ayesha pronunciation with local families; do not impose one universal pronunciation.
- [ ] Confirm pictures distinguish meanings; do not teach a word only by guessing from its picture.
- [ ] Check goals, prerequisites, all five check items, correct answers and meaningful fresh retry forms for every lesson.
- [ ] Record and sign off every English/Pashto clip, check no added schwa and align every read-along/phoneme sequence.
- [ ] Test children with low English exposure; observe accessibility alternatives and the 5–8 minute session target.
- [ ] Phase 2 reviewers must test all acceptance criteria on low-end Android, interrupted/offline sessions, mute and rapid repeated taps.
- [ ] All content/audio/assets remain pending review until named reviewers sign dated fields; no automatic status promotion.

## Phase 2 acceptance plan (not implementation)

1. Traverse the whole core path: assert each independent word’s GPCs and explicitly taught exception parts precede its use. Check spoken vocabulary is introduced before pictures are scored.
2. Verify exactly one skill goal, at least three distinct interaction types, no adjacent same type, five scored items, >=80%, required construction, and practice complete.
3. Close after each interaction, during speech and during animation: reopen at the same activity/cursor without double points or automatic success.
4. Rapidly tap English, Pashto and replay: exactly one clip and current callback owner; pause/mute must stop it.
5. Disable internet, complete activities, reconnect: local saves survive, outbox events merge idempotently, per-child profiles remain separate.
6. Check >=48dp targets, tap-dots alternative, captions, word taps, reduced motion and low-end Android fallback.
7. Verify parent-gated placement does not imply mastery from class selection or watching.
8. Native reviewers must approve all content and audio; the design audit is not their sign-off.
